## 3. The yaw residue: an out-of-trim body, not a bug

| subdivisions | panels | C[roll,yaw] | W*(x_G - x_B) | C[pitch,yaw] | W*(y_G - y_B) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1280 | 9.3921e+04 | 9.3921e+04 | 9.3921e+04 | 9.3921e+04 |
| 4 | 5120 | 9.4531e+04 | 9.4531e+04 | 9.4531e+04 | 9.4531e+04 |

Trimmed control (centre of gravity moved to the centre of buoyancy (1.2, 0.7)): C[roll,yaw] = 2.789e-08, C[pitch,yaw] = 1.213e-08 = 1.24e-14 of the matrix scale.

The couplings sit in the yaw *column* only: the transpose row stays at round-off, so `C` is **not symmetric** for an out-of-trim body. That is not a defect of the route but a property of the state it linearises: the equilibrium load is not zero, and it is carried round by a rotation about the vertical axis (which leaves the pressure field `p(z)` invariant) but not by a rotation about a horizontal one. A non-zero equilibrium load is not a potential, so there is no Hessian and no symmetry. The trimmed control above is symmetric to round-off.

The coupling is the equilibrium moment of a body that is not in trim, carried round by the rotation: the pressure route linearises the body it was handed, while the closed-form waterplane table assumes a trimmed body and has no such entry. Neither route is wrong; the verification body was not in trim.
