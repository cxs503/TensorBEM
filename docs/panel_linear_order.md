## 1. Case and analytic reference

- field: point-source Rankine potential `u = 1/(4 pi |x - x_s|)`, `x_s = (np.float64(0.0), np.float64(0.0), np.float64(0.5))` (inside the unit sphere)
- geometry: inscribed icosphere, radius 1, flat triangular panels
- readings: Dirichlet-to-flux (`(diag(c) - D) u = -S q` solved for `q`) and Neumann-to-potential (solved for `u`)
- panels per level: [80, 320, 1280, 5120]

## 2. Method

- one assembly per mesh feeds both readings, so the two errors share the same
  discrete operator; `h = sqrt(4 pi / N)` is the mean panel size
- panels that contain the collocation vertex use the closed-form self integral;
  panels nearer than twice their own size are refined toward the collocation
  point before quadrature, which is what makes the double-layer row sums exact
- the free term is the continuous `c = 1/2`; the row sum of `D` gives the cone
  solid angle instead, which differs by an `O(h)` cone defect -- reported as a
  diagnostic and used as a negative control

## 5. Notes, limits, applicability

- The measured order is a property of *this* scheme on *this* geometry. Flat
  facets represent a curved surface with `O(h**2)` positional error but with a
  piecewise-constant normal, so the flux datum itself is only `O(h)` accurate;
  the slopes above are reported as measured, not as hoped for.
- Vertex collocation on a polyhedron has an `O(h)` cone defect: the row sum of `D`
  is not `-1/2`.  The continuous `1/2` free term is the consistent choice (the
  free term implied by an exact field converges to `1/2`), and the row-sum
  variant is kept as a negative control.
- The energy inter-verification is the first-order damping identity, not `R_AW`:
  added resistance needs a second-order drift operator the repository withholds.
- Absolute numbers here are surface operator errors on a sphere at unit radius;
  they say nothing about hull-scale model error, which no mesh refinement removes.
