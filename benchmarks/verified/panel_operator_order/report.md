## 0. Headline

- **Operator order (constant panels, sphere, N = 80 ... 320):** flux slope +1.83 in `h` (-0.91 vs `log N`), surface-potential slope +1.92 in `h` (-0.96 vs `log N`).
- **Absolute accuracy at `N = 320`:** flux 1.149% and potential 0.989% of the closed-form reference.
- **Self panel integral:** repository polar rule vs closed form = 1.06e-15 (gate 1e-6); a coarse-rule control is 13.4% off, so the gate is live.

- **Verdicts:** C1a FAIL, C1b NOT_SUPPORTED, C2 PASS, C3 PASS, C4 PASS, C5 PASS. C1a fails *upwards*: the scheme is faster than the
  seeded band, which is discussed in section 8.

## 1. Case and analytic reference

- field: point-source Rankine potential u = 1/(4*pi*|x - x_s|)
- source position `x_s = (0.0, 0.0, 0.5)` (inside the unit sphere)
- geometry: icosphere, radius 1, vertices on the sphere, flat panels
- maps measured: exterior Dirichlet -> fluid-normal flux q_e = -du/dn_body; exterior Neumann -> surface potential u
- panels per level: [80, 320]

## 2. Method

One assembly per mesh (`assemble_surface_matrices`) feeds both readings, so the
two errors share the same discrete operator. `h = sqrt(4*pi/N)` is the mean panel
size; the order is the least-squares slope of `log(error)` vs `log(h)` over all
levels. The quadrature axis re-solves one mesh at several Duffy orders, which
separates a quadrature floor from the discretisation order.

## 5. Honest scope

- `free_surface_green_function`: REAL (src/tensorbem/free_surface3d.py::green_function_3d) Rankine + image + wave kernel; used by the 3-D radiation solver
- `constant_panel_biem`: REAL (src/tensorbem/solver3d.py::assemble_surface_matrices)
- `linear_panel_operator`: NOT_SUPPORTED (src/tensorbem/solver3d.py) constant-panel collocation only; no P1 basis or P1 assembly exists
- `kochin_far_field_damping`: REAL (src/tensorbem/free_surface_solver3d.py::far_field_damping_matrix_3d)
- `added_resistance_far_field`: NOT_SUPPORTED (src/tensorbem/ice_rao.py::ADDED_RESISTANCE_STATUS) second-order drift-force operator absent; repository declares it withheld

the seeded band -0.5 +- 0.075 is read against the panel count (error ~ N**-1/2 is the classical O(h) of a constant-panel scheme in 3-D; an O(h**2) scheme would give -1.0, the band quoted for linear panels). The order in h is twice the N slope and is reported alongside.

The seeded band assumes a geometry-limited h**(-1/2) rate.  The measured centroid-collocation rate of this constant-panel scheme is reported as it came out of the regression, together with the per-step orders; a slope steeper than the band means the band understates the scheme, not that the scheme is wrong.

## 8. Pitfalls, limits and what this does not show

1. **The measured order beats the seeded band.** The campaign band (`-0.5` in the
   panel-count convention) assumes a geometry-limited `h**0.5` rate. The constant-
   panel centroid-collocation scheme measured here is second order in `h` on this
   smooth closed geometry for both maps. The band is therefore reported as FAIL
   rather than re-banded: the number is the number, and the *reason* for the gap
   (centroid sampling averaging the first-order density error, plus an `O(h**2)`
   geometry defect) is a hypothesis, not a proof -- no error analysis was carried
   out here. What *is* excluded is a broken operator: the errors shrink, the
   solid-angle identity holds to round-off, and the coarse-quadrature control is
   red on the same gate.
2. **One geometry, one field.** A sphere with an on-axis interior source is smooth,
   convex and free of edges and waterlines. Sharp edges, waterline corners and
   surface-piercing geometries are exactly where constant-panel schemes lose order,
   and none of them is measured here (the campaign node asked for the sphere).
3. **No order-1 reference.** The order-1 half of the criterion is untested: there is
   no P1 assembly in `src/`. The constant-panel slope landing near `-1.0` in the
   N convention must not be read as 'linear panels are unnecessary' -- the P1 rate
   is simply not measured.
4. **The energy identity is first order.** `R_AW` needs a second-order drift-force
   operator that does not exist in this repository. `B_near` vs `B_far` tests the
   same operator pair (pressure integral vs Kochin integral) at the order the code
   supports, and nothing here validates an added-resistance number.
5. **Single frequency, single hull for the identity.** The `ka` probe below shows
   the residual is smooth in `ka` at one coarse mesh; it is not a proof that the
   identity holds at every frequency or for lifting/viscous-flow hybrids.
